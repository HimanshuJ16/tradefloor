# Market makers and option writers on ^NSEI, horizon 1m (what-if: W1)

## What we see
- E1/E6: The 1,000+ point sell-off already had rising crude, US-Iran tensions and rising US yields as drivers; a surprise 25bp repo hike on 7 Oct piles a domestic tightening shock on top of an already deteriorating external backdrop — this stops being a purely technical air-pocket and becomes a genuine repricing event.
- W1: A hike (vs. the prior "held, neutral stance" at the August review) is a hawkish surprise landing on an oversold, weak-rupee, high-yield tape. That combination — surprise + fragile starting point — is exactly what blows out realized and implied vol and widens the range we have to quote around, so we treat it differently from a routine short-term overshoot.
- Already in the price / E4: Before the hike, the index was deeply oversold (RSI 27.7, %B -0.01) and testing the 200-week MA — the kind of setup we'd normally fade hard. A confirmed hike on top of that muddies the fade: some of the move into early October is a legitimate repricing of discount rates, not just crowded one-way flow we can lean against.
- E3/E5/E7: Broad breadth damage, the 12% drawdown from highs, and weak risk appetite (NSE's own listing) all describe a market already short of dip-buyers; a hike removes one more reason for that buyer to show up on the next leg down, so our liquidity provision has to be more defensive around the announcement.
- Backdrop: rising US 10-year yields, a weak rupee and elevated oil were already headwinds; a domestic hike compounds the rupee/yield story rather than offsetting it, reinforcing that this is a macro shock, not noise.

## What we do
Around the 7 October decision and the 8 October session we get more defensive, not more aggressive: we widen quotes, pull back size on the bid into the initial post-hike move, and hedge gamma more actively rather than leaning hard into the dip the way we would on a pure technical overshoot. Once the immediate repricing is absorbed over a session or two, our underlying fade instinct reasserts on whatever residual oversold condition remains, but the conviction to buy the initial drop is lower than in the baseline because this catalyst is real, not just crowded flow.

## What would change our mind
If the hike is accompanied by dovish forward guidance (signaling a pause) or the index finds support quickly without breaking the 52-week low, we'd treat it as a one-off adjustment already priced in and go back to leaning into oversold dips with baseline conviction. If instead the hike triggers disorderly follow-through — a rupee air-pocket, a yield spike, or a break of 22,331 with no bounce — we'd stay defensive well past two days and keep spreads wide rather than fade.
