## What we see
- E1: Unchanged from baseline — crude, US-Iran tensions and rising US bond yields are explicitly named alongside FPI outflows as today's drivers. This is our core allocation trigger regardless of the RBI decision.
- E2: Rs 5-6 lakh crore lost confirms outflows are already running.
- E6: Oil, yields and geopolitics are flagged to persist near-term — the backdrop W1 lands into doesn't improve on its own.
- W1: A 25bp hike to 5.50% on Oct 7, against a backdrop of an already-falling, oversold index, rising crude, rising US yields, and a weakening rupee, reads two ways for us. On the currency side, it's a modest positive: defending INR carry when the dollar index is up and USD/INR has already drifted to 95.98 reduces one input to the outflow calculus — a central bank visibly protecting the currency lowers FX-driven redemption risk on our book. On the equity side, it's a negative: tightening into a market that's already down 5.4% on the month and testing its 200-week MA raises the domestic discount rate right as growth-sensitive sectors (banks, autos, real estate) are under pressure, and a hike explicitly framed as reactive to external pressure (oil, US yields) rather than domestic overheating reads as a central bank behind the curve, not ahead of it.
- Earnings season (TCS Oct 8, HCLTech Oct 12, HDFC/Axis Oct 17, Infosys Oct 23) now opens one day after the hike — bank earnings in particular will show the first read of margin/NIM impact, which we'll weight heavily for country-allocation revisions.
- E3/E4/E5/E7: Unchanged from baseline — breadth damage, technical drawdown levels and the NSE listing are secondary to the macro/rates calculus.

## What we do
The hike doesn't resolve our core problem — US yields and crude are still rising faster than anything RBI can offset with 25bp — so we don't rotate back in on this alone. It marginally reduces currency-driven outflow urgency but adds a fresh growth/earnings headwind just as the reporting season starts, so on net we stay underweight and slightly extend our horizon for re-engagement, watching bank earnings for confirmation of margin damage before revising country weight either way.
