# Domestic mutual funds and insurers on ^NSEI, horizon 1m (what-if: W1)

## What we see
- E1: A 1,000+ point Sensex drop on oil/US-Iran tensions/bond yields/FPI outflows is exactly the kind of foreign-flow-driven air-pocket our SIP book is built to absorb. We watch it, we don't panic-sell into it.
- E2: Rs 5-6 lakh crore of notional wealth wiped out in a session is a headline number, not a fundamentals change. Doesn't move our valuation view on its own.
- E3: 40% of Nifty constituents down >20% from highs and deteriorating breadth is the kind of dispersion that gives our fundamental desks stock-specific entry points in quality names — we screen for babies thrown out with the bathwater here.
- E4: Nifty testing the 200-week moving average for the first time in six years is a textbook long-horizon support test — our mandate's scaling-in zone, but a rate hike right at this level raises the bar for how fast we scale in.
- E5: -12% from the September 2024 peak is a meaningful drawdown but not yet a level that changes our multi-year fair-value math on India Inc.
- E6/E7: Macro overhang (oil, US-Iran, yields) and a weak NSE listing debut are near-term sentiment drags, not earnings-power changes. E7 is discounted almost entirely.
- Already in the price: RSI14 27.7, stochastic near 2, price at the lower Bollinger band, index on its 200-week MA — oversold and support-tested, the setup our mandate is built to buy, tempered by the ADX-confirmed downtrend and rising-yield/rising-oil backdrop.
- W1 (what-if): A surprise 25bp hike to 5.50% on 7 October, from an RBI that had been holding with a neutral stance, lands directly inside our deployment window and right after global (US) yields have already been rising 11-19% over 1-3m. It raises our domestic discount rate on future earnings at the exact moment we were leaning into a valuation dip, and it compounds the rupee-weakening, oil-import-bill pressure already flagged in the backdrop. A hike our desks did not have priced as the base case (the seed states the August stance was neutral/hold) reads as a genuine hawkish surprise, not confirmation of an already-expected path — it makes rate-sensitive names (banks' cost of funds, autos, real estate, leveraged corporates) less attractive relative to a week ago, even as it signals the RBI is prioritizing the rupee/inflation over growth support just as the index tests long-term support.

## What we do
The SIP engine keeps running — that flow is structural, not a switch we flip on a policy surprise — but our discretionary top-up buying into this dip slows markedly until we see the post-policy commentary and Governor's tone on 7 October. We shift what we do buy toward rate-insensitive quality (consumer staples, IT exporters helped by a weaker rupee, pharma) and pull back from bank/NBFC/rate-sensitive names we'd otherwise have been accumulating at the 200-week MA. Net-net we stay net buyers because the SIP book never really stops, but with less conviction and a smaller increment than we'd have committed absent the hike.

## What would change our mind
A dovish press conference tone alongside the hike (framed as a one-and-done inflation-anchoring move, not the start of a cycle) would let us resume full-conviction dip-buying quickly. A second consecutive hike signal, a rupee that keeps weakening despite the higher repo rate, or crude staying above $90-95 into Q2 earnings would push our fair-value estimate down further and stretch our deployment pace out well past this one-month horizon.
