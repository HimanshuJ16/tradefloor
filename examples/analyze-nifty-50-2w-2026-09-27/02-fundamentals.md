## Read
The macro backdrop into this two-week window is stacked against NIFTY: the index is already below its 200-day average and down mid-single-digits over both 1 and 3 months, while the two biggest swing factors for Indian equities — oil and US yields — have both moved sharply against it (crude +12% in a month, US 10Y yield +11% in a month), which squeezes the current account, pressures the rupee (already weakening), and raises the cost of capital for a market that leans on foreign portfolio flows. VIX is up 15% on the month even though its absolute level and percentile are unremarkable, consistent with a market pricing incremental downside risk rather than panic. None of this is a valuation call — there's no company-level data for an index — and two weeks is short enough that any of these macro variables (especially oil) could reverse without notice, so the read is directional and macro-driven, not fundamental in the equity-analysis sense.

## What would change this view
- A reversal in crude oil (a pullback toward $80s WTI) would remove the single biggest headwind
- US 10Y yield stabilizing or rolling over would ease EM outflow pressure
- Rupee stabilizing or strengthening against the dollar would signal the FII outflow pressure is easing
