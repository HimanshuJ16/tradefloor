## Read
The tape is genuinely stressed, not just noisy: multiple independent outlets confirm a seven-week losing streak, the longest since the 2020 Covid crash, driven by three concrete and still-live external forces — a 12%-in-a-month crude spike, a sharp global bond selloff (US 10Y near 5.2%), and reported FII outflows compounding rupee weakness. Notably the S&P 500 is up over the same month and above its 200-day average, so this reads as an India/EM-specific repricing rather than a global risk-off event, and a strong GDP print was reportedly sold rather than bought because it dimmed near-term rate-cut hopes just as inflationary inputs (oil) were rising. The one credible offsetting catalyst is J.P. Morgan's reported $115B inflow thesis pitching India as an AI-trade hedge, plus a still-low VIX (46th percentile) that argues against panic pricing. The RBI MPC meeting on 5-7 October sits squarely inside this horizon and is the single most consequential scheduled catalyst: a hold-with-hawkish-tone given oil-driven inflation risk would extend the current pressure, while any dovish signal or growth-supportive language could trigger the JPM-style flow reversal.

## What would change this view
- A dovish or growth-supportive tone from the 5-7 October RBI MPC, or any signal that rate-cut odds are back on the table
- Crude oil rolling over from current highs (~$92 WTI) or the US 10Y yield stabilizing/reversing, which are the two macro drivers cited across every material headline
- Concrete FII inflow data (rather than just the J.P. Morgan narrative) confirming the reported $115B thesis is actually showing up in flows
